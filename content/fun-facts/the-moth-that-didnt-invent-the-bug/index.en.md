**A moth, a logbook, and a famous line: "First actual case of bug being found". The object is real. The word's origin story is a legend.**

## What's true

On 9 September 1947, at 3:45 pm, the team running the Harvard Mark II, an electromechanical calculator, found a moth stuck in relay #70 of panel F. It was removed and taped to the logbook page with a caption. That logbook, moth included, is now kept at the Smithsonian's National Museum of American History.

## What's not

Two myths go around.

- **"Grace Hopper found the moth."** She was part of the Mark II team, but the Smithsonian notes the logbook was probably not hers. Mostly she helped make the story famous by telling it.
- **"It's where the word 'bug' comes from."** Look at the caption: *first **actual** case*. The first **real** one. The joke only works if small faults were already called "bugs".

## An already old word

In 1878, Thomas Edison wrote in a letter that small faults and difficulties like these are called "bugs". The Oxford English Dictionary also cites, for this meaning, an 1889 *Pall Mall Gazette* article about Edison. The word was in engineers' vocabulary seventy years before the Harvard moth.

## What to take from it

A good story travels better than a good source. When an anecdote is too perfect, it's worth going back to the original document: here, all it takes is reading the word "actual" in the caption.
